HUMCOM Reflection

Coming into this Human-Computer Interaction course I honestly thought it would mostly be a coding refresher. I had not really touched HTML and CSS since grade 7 so it was nice to get back into that. However I quickly realized that HCI is about way more than just writing code to make things look cool. It is an interdisciplinary field that focuses on the interfaces between us and the computers we use everyday. The biggest reality check for me was realizing that not everyone is as tech literate as I am. I cannot just assume the user knows what to do or how to troubleshoot. I learned that there is a huge difference between usability which is just about efficiency and accessibility which ensures people with disabilities can actually use the product. This perspective shift made me want to build software that is easily understandable for everyone and not just for tech experts like myself.

To achieve a good UI/UX, we learned about core interaction design principles. We studied concepts like affordance which tells the user how an element works without needing explicit instructions. We also covered consistency which helps users predict what will happen based on their previous interactions. One of the most interesting parts for me was learning about our own biases in design. We discussed the aesthetic-usability effect where research shows we naturally think beautiful designs are more usable even if they are actually broken or hard to use. This taught me a valuable lesson. I need to look past the visuals to ensure the core design actually works well for the human using it.

Finally we looked at how technology is becoming ubiquitous. Devices like smartwatches and fitness bands are everywhere now and designing for them is totally different than designing for a phone screen. A huge topic that really stuck with me though was our class debate on AI. We argued whether the societal and environmental impact of AI is actually worth it. We also touched on the dangers of AI bias and how these systems can make unfair judgments or generalize data. It made me realize that as developers we are responsible for the ethical side of tech too. This course has genuinely given me a new lens through which to view the digital world. Thank you Ma'am Graeven for being such a huge part of my journey. You helped me look past the code to see the human side of technology and I am grateful for the guidance you gave me this semester.
